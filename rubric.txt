Pattern B — Saw a broken process first-hand, fixed it without being asked, and peers adopted the fix.
  - Rohan: built an Excel shipment tracker because the team "had no reliable way to know where each shipment
    stood" — adopted by the 12-person ops team in two weeks. Later built a BL verification prototype over a

Pattern C — Names their own misses and carries ownership under pressure with no layer above.
  - Lavanya: killed 2 of her own features on usage data; wrote the outage post-mortem (internal and customer
    versions) and owned follow-ups to closure. Review note: "She doesn't hedge."
  - Aditya: ran and shared a post-mortem on a 4-month deal he lost; it became standard practice.
  - Rohan: led a vendor migration under time pressure; "translate field requirements into engineering spec
    without a product layer".
  - Meghna: "no escalation to management in the last 14 months"; fixed the customs hold "before the client
    became aware".
  - Sunita: independent consultant, owns engagements end-to-end with no manager.
  Contrast: Vikram, Rahul and Preetham list only wins — no killed work, no post-mortem, no failure owned.
  Vikram and Preetham worked inside layered teams (4-person PM team presenting to CTO; 12-engineer team).

NOT USED AS SIGNAL: years of experience, titles, MBA/college tier, certifications (both Exceeds and

======================================================================
STEP 2 — RUBRIC
Each criterion scored 1–5. Weighted total = sum(score/5 * weight) → 0–100.
======================================================================

------------------------------ PM ------------------------------

1. Operations-floor time
What a strong candidate looks like: Has held a role where they personally handled shipments, documents,
carriers, customs, warehouse or dispatch work day to day — names concrete volumes, counterparties (CHAs,
shipping lines, carriers, port/ICD) or incidents. 5 = 1+ year doing the operational work itself in
freight/logistics/supply chain. 3 = adjacent ops (field ops, marketplace ops, supply ops in another industry)
or embedded with ops users for weeks. 1 = only desk, consulting, strategy or API-level contact with operations.
Source: Rohan, Sunita, Aditya, Meghna, Lavanya (present); Vikram, Preetham, Rahul (absent).
Weight: 30%

2. Unprompted fix, adopted by peers
What a strong candidate looks like: The CV describes a problem they noticed themselves, a fix they built
without being assigned it (sheet, tool, process, prototype), and evidence others adopted it (team/branch
standard, N users, retained permanently). 5 = clear self-initiated fix with named adoption. 3 = built something
new but it was their assigned scope, or adoption not stated. 1 = only delivered assigned work.
Source: Rohan (tracker, BL prototype), Sunita (weekend workflow), Lavanya (triage, dashboard).
Weight: 25%

3. Names own misses
What a strong candidate looks like: Explicitly records something that failed or was cut, and what they did
about it — killed a feature on data, post-mortem written and shared, reversed their own earlier call.
5 = specific kill/post-mortem with the action taken. 3 = mentions iteration or learning without a concrete
failure. 1 = CV contains only wins.
Source: Lavanya (killed 2 features, outage post-mortem), Aditya (lost-deal post-mortem).
Weight: 20%

4. Owns it with no layer above
What a strong candidate looks like: Was the sole/first owner of an area — no manager in their function making
the calls — and decisions were theirs end to end. 5 = sole owner, reported to founder/CEO/business head, made
calls themselves. 3 = owned a module inside a team with senior PMs/leads above. 1 = supported others' decisions.
Source: Lavanya (sole PM), Rohan (no product layer), Aditya (no AM layer), Meghna (no escalation in 14 months).
Weight: 15%

5. Holds under pressure
What a strong candidate looks like: Describes a specific live incident (customs hold, outage, vendor change,
peak volume, deadline) that they personally resolved, with the outcome. 5 = named incident, their action,
outcome. 3 = general "high-pressure environment" claim with some evidence. 1 = nothing.
Source: Meghna (overnight customs hold), Sunita (vendor format change), Rohan (migration under pressure).
Weight: 10%

PM total: 100%

------------------------------ SPM ------------------------------
Same criteria; bar is higher, especially on independence.

1. Operations-floor time
What a strong candidate looks like: Has personally done day-to-day operational work in freight/logistics/supply
chain (shipments, documents, carriers, customs, port/ICD, warehouse), AND that experience shaped a product or
system decision they can point to (e.g. "designed X because I knew what the data actually contained from my time in terminal ops").
5 = multi-year ops floor time + a product/system decision that came directly from it. 3 = ops floor time with no
link to later decisions, or deep adjacent ops. 1 = no operational exposure.
Source: Rohan (CHA ops → tracking platform), Lavanya (carrier ops → carrier-integration product).
Weight: 25%

2. Unprompted fix, adopted by peers
What a strong candidate looks like: Self-initiated change that spread beyond their own team — to the company,
other regions, or customers — or reshaped how a function works. 5 = cross-team/company-wide adoption of
something they started unasked. 3 = adopted within their own team only. 1 = assigned work only.
Source: Rohan (BL module became a core platform feature), Lavanya (dashboard adopted by 2 other teams).
Weight: 20%

3. Names own misses
What a strong candidate looks like: Has killed or reversed something significant — including their own idea —
and owned the consequences publicly (post-mortem, customer communication). 5 = killed own idea / major
reversal with documented follow-through. 3 = killed others' features or minor reversals. 1 = only wins.
Source: Lavanya, Aditya.
Weight: 20%

4. Owns it with no layer above
What a strong candidate looks like: Has run a product area or function with nobody senior in that function
above them for 2+ years, reporting to a founder/CEO, making architectural or business calls alone and living
with them. 5 = led the function/area solo for 2+ years with decisions they defend. 3 = sole owner of an area
for under 2 years, or senior IC under a Head of Product. 1 = worked within a layered PM org.
Source: Rohan ("most senior engineer in the room"), Sunita (independent), Lavanya (sole PM).
Weight: 25%

5. Holds under pressure
What a strong candidate looks like: Describes a specific high-stakes incident — platform-level outage, migration,
or customer-threatening failure — that they personally led to resolution, with the outcome. 5 = led resolution of a high-stakes incident end to end.
3 = contributed to one. 1 = nothing.
Source: Rohan (vendor migration, no data loss), Lavanya (4-hour outage).
Weight: 10%

SPM total: 100%

======================================================================
SCORING RULES
- Score only on what the CV text shows. No credit for titles, years, certifications, college or tools.
- Every score must carry a one-line reason quoting or paraphrasing the CV line it rests on.
- Buzzwords without an outcome ("strategic enabler", "orchestrated", "pioneered") score as absent.
- Every candidate is scored on BOTH rubrics regardless of the role applied for.
